## Part B — The out-of-scope discovery

### The prompt (paste verbatim into your document)

> *Thirty minutes before your report is due, you notice the SSRF you used also resolves a second internal host that is NOT in your engagement — and the response suggests that host is live in production and reachable from the internet right now. What do you do?*

### Your response (300–400 words — walk through all four points)

#### 1. What you verify first — and why — without exceeding scope a second time
[Name the *minimum* verification that confirms the risk without you reaching deeper into the out-of-scope host. *Example shape: "I confirm only what I can see from evidence I already hold — the resolved hostname and the response banner already in front of me — and I do not send a single new request to that host. My D1 reconstruction taught me that touching a system and demonstrating reach are different acts; I have reach, I do not need to touch."* Tie it to a specific lesson from your D1/D3 work.]

#### 2. Whether you stop your report to disclose — to whom, and how urgently
[State the decision plainly: a live, internet-reachable production host is a now-problem, not a footnote. Name the role you go to, the channel, and the urgency. *Example: "Yes — I pause assembly and disclose immediately to the engagement lead and Sankofa's security contact, by phone or page, not email, because 'reachable from the internet right now' does not wait for a 9 a.m. inbox."*]

#### 3. What you do NOT do
[At least three specific refusals: do you exploit it? Screenshot its contents? Sit on it to make a bigger, more impressive finding? *"I do not exploit it. I do not pull data from it to strengthen my report. I do not hold it back as a 'bonus finding' for a follow-on engagement — sitting on a live exposure to make my report look better is the exact inversion of the job."* Each refusal names its reason.]

#### 4. Exactly what you write in the disclosure — and the four-day clock
[A one-line version of the disclosure message, plus how you handle the deadline. *Example: "Message: 'Out-of-scope but urgent: the SSRF also resolves <host>, which appears to be a production system reachable from the public internet. I have not probed it further. Recommend immediate triage.' On the clock: I note the discovery and disclosure time in my report, deliver the in-scope report on time, and flag the new issue as an addendum rather than letting the deadline pressure me into either ignoring it or quietly exceeding scope to investigate it."*]
