[**Cause, not symptom.** *Example (SQLi): "The data-access layer concatenates the raw `username` POST value directly into the SQL string — `...WHERE username = '" . $username . # PART 2 — The board brief (one page, plain language)

*Audience: a CISO who will quote you to the board. No jargon. No CVE numbers, no MITRE IDs, no CVSS vector in this section — those live in Part 1. If a non-technical director cannot follow a sentence, rewrite it.*

### What happened, in plain terms
[2–3 sentences. *Example shape: "An attacker walked into a forgotten internal admin tool that had been online and unmaintained since 2019, tricked it into letting them in without a password, and from there reached the keys to our customer database. They did not need to break encryption or steal an employee's laptop — they used the front door we left unlocked."*]

### What was at risk — in money-and-risk terms
[Tie it to the real numbers. *Example: "84,210 customer records — names, emails, account numbers, balances — were one query away. Under the NDPA, a breach of this size is a reportable event with regulatory exposure, mandatory customer notification, and the reputational cost of telling 84,210 people their bank details were reachable. The cloud credentials the attacker obtained would have let them read that entire database, not just a sample."* Anchor every claim to a real artefact — the `05-exfil-sample.csv` field shape, the 84,210 figure, the IAM blast radius.]

### The one control I recommend funding
[Name **one** control. Then — this is the discriminator — name **the one Stage 2 log line or payload it WOULD have stopped, and one it would NOT.**

*Example: "Fund the decommissioning (or, if it must live, the placement behind authenticated SSO + WAF + egress filtering) of the legacy-admin app. WOULD have stopped: the `02:15:31` `admin' OR '1'='1` login bypass — SSO in front means there is no app-local login to inject into. Would NOT have stopped: a valid employee with stolen SSO credentials; this control closes the unauthenticated front door, not credential theft, which needs MFA — a separate ask I am flagging, not funding here."*]
