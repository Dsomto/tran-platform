We have over 3,000 applications and the number is still rising. We can accept only about 500, possibly 600. This sheet is strict by design — the bar is high and most applications will not clear it. Work it in order. Any single trigger in Section A ends the review immediately. There is no waitlist and no "maybe": every application ends as Accept or Reject.

A

Automatic rejects — any one ends the review

Reject if any is true
  • × Full-time employed — with no availability case made. Full-time employment is disqualifying by default — the hours cannot be found twice. There is one exception, and it is judged in Section C: an applicant who makes a genuine, specific case for their availability inside their own write-up. Employed full-time with no such case = reject.
  • × The write-up is AI-generated. If the answer reads as machine-written — smooth, generic, polished but empty, no concrete personal detail — reject. We are accepting a person, not a prompt. Be ruthless here.
  • × The write-up is not personalized. If it could belong to any applicant — nothing specific about this person, their story, or why this programme — reject.
  • × The application does not ring true. Inconsistent, exaggerated, or unverifiable claims. If experience, goals and write-up do not line up into one honest picture, reject.
  • × No real full name. Strip any emoji or symbol from the name first. If what remains is not a plausible human name — gibberish, a single letter, "asdf" — reject.
  • × Cannot show genuine commitment. If the applicant's own words show they cannot realistically commit to and show up for the programme, reject. See Section C.
  • × Empty, gibberish, or one-line write-up. A blank, nonsensical or barely-there answer to the differentiator question is an automatic reject.
  • × Required fields blank, or a duplicate application. Experience and goals must both be completed. Same person applying twice — keep the stronger, reject the rest.
B

Required of every accepted applicant

All required — no partial pass
  • ✓ Genuinely available and committed. Either not in full-time employment, or — if employed — they made the full availability case in their write-up (Section C). Their own words must show they will give this programme real, consistent time.
  • ✓ A personalized, human-written write-up. Written in their own voice, specific to them, self-aware, clearly not AI-generated. It must read like a real person who genuinely wants this.
  • ✓ An honest, consistent application. Every part of the application lines up and rings true. Nothing exaggerated, nothing that cannot be believed.
  • ✓ A real, complete full name. First and last name, no emojis or symbols. This is the name that will appear on their certificate.
  • ✓ A genuine learning signal and real intent. Courses, labs, TryHackMe / Hack The Box, CTFs, self-study, projects. Beginners are welcome — being new is fine, showing no effort is not.
  • ✓ Clear, relevant goals and a track. Goals connect to cybersecurity and to a track — SOC, Ethical Hacking, or GRC. "Not sure yet" is acceptable; the track is assigned later.

Every point above is required. Miss one and the applicant is rejected — there is no partial pass.

C

Commitment & availability — the core gate

Judge this hardest
The time an applicant can give matters more than anything else. This programme is intensive. The single biggest reason a person fails it is that they could not give it the hours. Judge commitment hard, from two things — the application has no separate weekly-hours question, so use these:
  • Their status. Students, unemployed applicants, freelancers and people with flexible schedules can commit — judge the rest of the application. Full-time employment is disqualifying by default.
  • Their own words. Read what they wrote about themselves and their goals. Vague, casual or non-committal → reject. Accept only applicants who clearly will show up and put in the work.
The one exception — full-time employed

A full-time-employed applicant may still be accepted — but only if their own write-up makes the case, and makes it fully. It must do all four:

  1. state plainly that they currently have a job;
  2. say what the job actually is;
  3. explain why they want this internship and what they expect from it;
  4. show convincingly that the job still leaves them free enough to fully commit to the programme.

If even one of those is missing, or the case is not convincing, reject. A bare "I have a job but I'm interested" is not a case.
