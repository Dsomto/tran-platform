# Applicant update email — "while you wait"

Sent to everyone who applied, before decisions go out. Swap `{First name}` for
the merge field and drop in your real LinkedIn profile link.

Key dates: decision **on or before 26 May**, onboarding **1 June**.

---

## Subject line (pick one)

- An update on your application 🛡️
- Your application — plus 3 things to do before June
- We've got your application. Here's what happens next.

## Body

Hi {First name},

A lot of people say they want to get into cybersecurity. Far fewer actually do
something about it. You did — you applied. So this one's for you.

Here's where things stand: we're reading every application properly — really
reading them, not skimming a pile. That's slower, and we're fine with that;
you deserve a real look. You'll get your decision **on or before 26 May**. If
you're in, **onboarding starts 1 June**, and the programme kicks off from
there. Watch this inbox — every update lands here.

Now, the wait. Most "we'll get back to you" emails stop here. Ours doesn't —
here are three things actually worth doing before June:

**Play, and be loud about it.** Head to the **Games** section and take on
**CyberWordle**. It's security made genuinely fun. Win it, screenshot it, post
it — and tag me on **LinkedIn**. I read those, and I reply. We're not just
running a programme; we're building a community, and the people in it talk to
each other. Start now.

**Get a small head start.** You don't need cybersecurity knowledge to join us
— that's the truth, and we mean it. But a little goes a long way. If you want
one solid, free place to begin, do **Cisco Networking Academy's "Introduction
to Cybersecurity."** A few evenings of it and you'll walk in on 1 June feeling
ready instead of nervous.

**Pull a friend in.** Quietly — applications are closing **earlier than we
planned**. The response has been bigger than we expected. If you know someone
sharp, curious, the type who asks "wait, how do hackers actually do that" —
tell them today. Not next week. The window is shrinking.

One more thing, while it's on my mind: if you run a business, freelance, or
you're into red teaming, take a look at **scanone.ethnoscyber.com**. It's a
free risk-exposure platform — it scans for vulnerabilities, shows you exactly
where you're exposed, and helps you close the gaps. Register and it keeps you
on cyber updates too. If offensive security is your lane, you'll have fun with
it.

That's everything. Thank you for applying — truly. Talk soon.

— Somto
Ubuntu Bridge Initiative
