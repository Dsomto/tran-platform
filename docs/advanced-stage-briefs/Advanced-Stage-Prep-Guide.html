Preparation Guide

How to spend the break week so you arrive ready for the Advanced Stage.

The Advanced Stage is hands-on and it moves quickly. You will build and test real systems on your own computer and show your results, so the people who prepare during the break start well ahead. You have two jobs this week: get your machine set up, and sharpen the fundamentals for the track you are taking. Read the section for your track, learn the words you do not yet know, and get a little practice in so the tools feel familiar. You are not expected to know everything. You are expected to arrive able to work.

Get your machine ready — everyone

Do this first. It always takes longer than people expect.

Your laptop
  • 8 GB RAM minimum, 16 GB recommended. You will run virtual machines.
  • About 100 GB of free disk space.
  • Virtualisation turned on in the BIOS/UEFI (VT-x or AMD-V). Check this now.
  • A reliable internet connection.
Install and get comfortable
  • A hypervisor for virtual machines: VirtualBox or VMware.
  • Docker, for tools that run in one command.
  • A Linux you can move around in. Ubuntu for defence and governance work; Kali as a virtual machine for offensive work.
  • Git, plus a code editor. Basic Python helps.
Free accounts to open
  • A GitHub account. You will publish some work as portfolio.
  • An AWS free-tier account.
  • A Cisco NetAcad account.
  • Practice accounts on TryHackMe and PortSwigger Web Security Academy.
The one skill worth most
  • Comfort at the Linux command line: moving around, reading files, piping with grep and awk, installing packages, running Docker. If you do one thing this week, make the terminal feel like home.
Pick the track you are aiming for and go deep on that one. The three sections that follow tell you what to study, the vocabulary to master, the tools to handle, and where to practise for free.
Track One
SOC Analyst · Blue Team

Choose this if you like investigating. You read logs and network traffic, find the attacker hiding in ordinary noise, build the monitoring that catches them, and work out exactly what happened after a breach. It rewards patience and an obsession with evidence.

What to focus on
  • Log analysis at scale. Reading and querying large volumes of logs, and telling real signals apart from noise and false alarms.
  • SIEM fundamentals. Bringing logs in, writing queries, building dashboards and alerts.
  • Detection engineering. Turning attacker behaviour into rules that fire on the right things and stay quiet on the rest.
  • Network traffic analysis. Reading packet captures and spotting command-and-control and data theft.
  • Endpoint and memory forensics. What runs on a machine, how malware hides, and how to pull evidence from a memory image.
  • Incident response. The standard response lifecycle and how to rebuild an attack timeline from the evidence.
  • Network fundamentals. VLANs, segmentation, firewalls and access control lists, and why they matter for defence.
  • The attacker playbook. The MITRE ATT&CK tactics and techniques, so you know what to hunt for.

Track Two
GRC Analyst · Governance, Risk & Compliance

Choose this if you like judgement and writing. You turn messy business reality into clear decisions, weigh risk honestly, read between the lines of vendor and audit paperwork, and produce documents that executives and regulators can act on. It rewards structure, scepticism and precision.

What to focus on
  • Security frameworks. The NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0 and ISO/IEC 27001:2022, especially the Annex A controls.
  • Risk management. Building a risk register; likelihood against impact; inherent against residual risk; the treatment choices; and simple quantification.
  • Audit and compliance. How a control is tested, what separates strong evidence from weak, and what a nonconformity is.
  • Vendor and third-party risk. Reading a SOC 2 report, a security questionnaire and a data processing agreement with a critical eye.
  • Privacy and breach law. The shape of GDPR, the Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023 and CCPA, and what actually triggers a breach notification.
  • Policy work. Finding the gap in a policy and writing control language that people can actually follow.

Track Three
Ethical Hacking · VAPT · Red Team

Choose this if you like taking things apart, with permission. You find the flaw, turn it into real impact, and prove it in a clean report. This is the most technically demanding track, so come in with genuine hands-on practice rather than theory alone.

What to focus on
  • Testing methodology. The full flow: reconnaissance, enumeration, exploitation, privilege escalation, post-exploitation, and reporting.
  • Web application security. The OWASP Top 10 (injection, broken access control, cross-site scripting, server-side request forgery, authentication and token flaws) and how to chain them together.
  • API security. The OWASP API Top 10, and testing authentication, authorisation and business logic.
  • Privilege escalation. Local escalation on Linux and Windows, through misconfigured permissions, services and scheduled tasks.
  • Active Directory. How it works and how it is attacked. Start this early. It is the steepest topic in the track.
  • Cloud basics. AWS identity and access management, and how a misconfiguration becomes a way in.
  • Scripting. Python and Bash, to automate your reconnaissance and package your exploits.
  • Reporting. Writing a finding with clear impact, evidence and a fix.

Your week, in order

Start withThenIf time allows
Get the machine ready: virtualisation on, a hypervisor and Docker installed, a Linux VM booting cleanly. Study your track and learn the vocabulary until you can explain each word in a sentence. Run three to five free labs on your track so the tools feel familiar before day one.
A last word. The Advanced Stage rewards people who can do the work and explain it, not people who memorised definitions. Every task ends in real evidence you produced and can stand behind. Arrive set up, arrive curious, and be ready to work things out under pressure.
